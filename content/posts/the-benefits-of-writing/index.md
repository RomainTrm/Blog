Have you ever experienced the sensation to understand something enough that you could explain it? But when you get the opportunity, you realize that your explanation is at best messy, or doesn't make sense at all?

In my case, the reason is often the same: it implies several ideas that seem cohesive as a whole, but as I can't express them simultaneously, my explanation lacks a proper structure and my arguments don't support each other well. Some elements need predicates to be accepted, so they need to be expressed first as a logical path, a kind of chain of thoughts.

Maybe you're used to write things down, 



- Are you writing down your thoughts ? Paper / blog / linkedin / code / others
- To me it feels like it is a tought exercise
- feeling of understanding and sometimes mastering a topic
- under impression that I could express/explain it to someone
- in reality it would be a messy demonstration, with a lot of back and forth
- reason: my ideas as a whole seems cohesive, but I cannot express them simultaneously
- writing things forces me to structure my thoughts, to give them a hierachy, a logical path : a chain of toughts
- some elements of my reasoning needs some predicates, so I need to express theses predicates first
- these logical transitions are usually missing at first
- sometimes it mades me realize that some "logical" connections I imagined are not logical, or completly wrong
- in such cases : my mental model is wrong
- this process is true for me when writing a blog post, it is also true when writing code
- recent example : learning the translator pattern with TEA - I struggled to made a correct mental model of this pattern by solely reading it, I solved it when I managed to code it by myself
- same process on production code : our mental model constently adjusts with the reality of the code
- questioning about intensive AI usage : are we still writing enough ? are we missing important design decisions by not hiting walls ? are our mental models drifting dangerously from reality ?
